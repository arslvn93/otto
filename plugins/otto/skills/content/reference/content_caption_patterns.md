# Caption Patterns — By Platform

**Template Type:** Reference doc Otto uses to write captions for short-form video posts.
**The rule:** The caption is the conversion layer. The video gets the watch; the caption gets the DM, the save, the follow.

---

## Instagram

**Length sweet spot:** 100-250 words. Gets cut at 2 lines with a "...more" — first sentence is everything.

**Structure:**
1. First line = hook or curiosity gap (mirror the video hook, don't repeat verbatim)
2. Line break
3. Context or backstory in 2-4 short paragraphs (one idea per line, line breaks between)
4. Line break
5. CTA — single, specific ask
6. Hashtags at the end (or in first comment)

**Style:**
- Short sentences. Line breaks every 1-2 sentences. White space is your friend.
- Emojis sparingly — 1-3 per caption, used like punctuation, not decoration.
- Specific over vague: "South Austin" beats "this area." "$50K over asking" beats "well over asking."
- Write like you'd text a friend who happens to be a buyer.

**Example structure:**

```
[Hook line that gets them past the "...more"]

[1-2 sentences of context. Why this matters. Who it's for.]

[The actual point or insight. Specific. Concrete.]

[Optional: a personal observation or example from your business.]

[CTA in one line.]

#hashtag #hashtag #hashtag
```

---

## TikTok

**Length sweet spot:** Under 150 characters. The caption isn't the show — the video is.

**Structure:**
- One punchy line that frames the video.
- Maybe a second line with the CTA.
- Hashtags inline at the end.

**Style:**
- Conversational. Lowercase often fine. Contractions always.
- Avoid hype words and emoji walls.
- Don't restate the video — extend it or set it up.

**Example:**

```
the real reason your offer keeps getting beat 👇

dm me if you want to talk strategy

#firsttimehomebuyer #austinrealestate #homebuying
```

## YouTube Shorts

**Length sweet spot:** 50-100 words.

**Title:** This is where most of the work goes. Title shows up in YouTube search and on the watch page. Front-load the keyword. "First-time buyer tips" should appear in the title if that's the topic.

**Description:** Short summary, then a CTA, then optional links. Hashtags work but matter less than on TikTok or Instagram.

**Style:**
- A touch more formal than TikTok.
- Searchable language — write like someone is typing a question into YouTube.

**Example title:** `3 Things to Check Before You Make an Offer (Austin Buyers)`

**Example description:**

```
Most buyers skip these three things and regret it later. Here's what I check on every offer I write.

DM me on Instagram @[handle] if you want to walk through your specific situation.

#realestate #firsttimehomebuyer #austin
```

---

## Facebook Reels

Mirror the Instagram caption with one shift: the audience skews slightly older and more local. Tone can be a touch warmer and more personal. Hashtags optional and used sparingly (3-5 max).

---

## LinkedIn

**Different game entirely.** LinkedIn rewards insight, not hooks.

**Structure:**
1. First line = the insight or takeaway, stated plainly.
2. Line break.
3. 2-4 paragraphs explaining the why, with specifics and numbers when possible.
4. Optional: a question or invitation to comment.
5. 0-3 hashtags. Full words only. No "#realtorlife" — use "#RealEstate" or "#Housing".

**Style:**
- Full sentences. No slang. No emojis (or one, max, in the body).
- Lead with the professional insight, not the hook.
- Personal stories are great if they land on a takeaway. Don't tell a story that goes nowhere.

**Example:**

```
The biggest mistake I see first-time buyers make has nothing to do with their offer.

It's that they treat the inspection as a formality. They've already emotionally bought the house. They skim the report, sign off, and find out about the foundation crack three months later.

I tell every buyer the same thing: the inspection isn't a step. It's a decision point.

If you're house-shopping in Austin this year, that one shift in mindset will save you more than the price negotiation.

#RealEstate #HomeBuying
```

## Common Caption Mistakes

- **Repeating the video verbatim.** The caption extends the video. It doesn't transcribe it.
- **Wall of emojis.** One or two, used like punctuation. Not decoration.
- **Hashtag stuffing.** 30 hashtags isn't a strategy. See `content_hashtag_strategy.md`.
- **Buried CTA.** If the ask is in paragraph four, no one sees it. CTA goes near the top or near the bottom — not in the middle.
- **Vague closes.** "Reach out!" "Let me know!" "Thoughts?" — these don't convert. "DM me 'BUYER' and I'll send you my pre-approval checklist" converts.
