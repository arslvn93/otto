# Short-Form Video — Master Template

**Template Type:** Reference doc Otto reads when building any short-form video script (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts).
**When to use:** Any time the agent runs the "Make Me Content" workflow.

---

## Why Short-Form Works for Real Estate

The bar isn't "polished." The bar is "useful."

Agents who post consistently win on short-form because every video is a free billboard to people in their market who don't know them yet. The agents who try to make every Reel look like a TV commercial post twice and quit. The ones who treat it like an Instagram Story with a tighter hook post 50 times and book listing appointments from it.

Otto's job is to make the writing part disappear so the agent only has to film and post.

---

## The Three-Part Structure

Every short-form video — 30, 60, or 90 seconds — follows the same shape:

**Hook (0-3 seconds)** — The reason someone doesn't swipe away. Specific, surprising, or directly addressed to the viewer.

**Value (3 sec – end-minus-3)** — The actual point. One idea, one frame. Don't try to teach two things in 45 seconds. Pick one.

**CTA (last 3 seconds)** — One ask. Not three. "DM me," "save this," "comment a word," or sometimes nothing at all.

---

## Pacing Rules by Length

**30 seconds.** One idea, one beat. ~75 words of script. No setup, no warm-up — open at full speed, deliver, close.

**60 seconds.** One idea, two or three beats. ~150 words. Room for a brief story or two examples.

**90 seconds.** One idea, three or four beats. ~225 words. You can teach something real. But every line still has to earn its place — no padding.

When a script feels long, cut from the middle. The hook and CTA carry the most weight; the middle is where rambling lives.

---

## What to Avoid

**Real-estate clichés.** No "nestled," "must-see," "won't last," "stunning," "dream home," "luxury living at its finest." If a phrase shows up in 90% of MLS descriptions, it's invisible.

**Music-only intros.** Three seconds of B-roll with no voice is three seconds of swipe-bait. Open with the hook.

**Fake urgency.** "Don't miss out!" "Last chance!" Viewers can smell it. If there's real urgency, name the specific thing (offer deadline, open house date, market shift).

**"Did you know..." openers.** Lazy. Replace with a specific number, a contrarian statement, or a direct callout.

**Generic "POV:" framing.** Only use POV if the video is literally a point-of-view shot of something. Otherwise it's a tic.

**Lists of more than 3.** Short-form can't carry a 5-point list. If the agent has five points, pick three. Or split into multiple videos.

**Reading off a script verbatim.** The script is a scaffold. The agent should sound like themselves, not like they're reading.

## Platform-Specific Format Notes

**Instagram Reels.** 9:16 vertical. 30-90 sec sweet spot. Caption can be long (gets cut at 2 lines with a "more"). Hashtags go at the end of the caption or in the first comment. Save and share signals matter more than likes.

**TikTok.** 9:16 vertical. 21-34 sec sweet spot for new accounts; 60+ once an account has rhythm. Caption stays short and punchy. Hashtags inline. Text overlay is expected, not optional.

**YouTube Shorts.** 9:16 vertical. Under 60 sec hits the algorithm sweetly. Title matters more than other platforms (it shows up in search). Description can be long but few people read it.

**Facebook Reels.** Mirror Instagram. Slightly older audience. Captions can be a touch more conversational.

**LinkedIn.** Vertical works but 1:1 square also fine. Lead with the professional insight, not the hook. Drop slang. No hashtags or 3 max, full-word only.

## The Voice Check

Before producing the final script, ask: would this agent actually say these words out loud, in this order, to a client at a coffee shop?

If a line is too written, too "marketing," or too polished — rewrite it the way they'd say it. The agent's voice profile (from Set Up Otto) is the source of truth. If the agent said they don't use exclamation marks, don't use them. If they said their tone is "luxury and elevated," the vocabulary shifts. If they said "casual and friendly," contractions are mandatory.

---

## What Makes a Reel Save-Worthy

Saves are the real signal — more than likes, more than comments. People save:

- Specific tactical advice they want to revisit ("3 things to check before you make an offer")
- Numbers and stats they want to reference later
- Lists ("5 questions to ask your lender")
- Visual guides ("what to look for in an inspection report")

Otto should bias toward this when picking topics. "Why I love this neighborhood" doesn't get saved. "The 3 questions I ask every seller before listing their home" does.
